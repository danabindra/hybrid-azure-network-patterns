## Four-Point Interconnect Model

The concept behind the four-point interconnect and similar highly avalable meshes was pioneered by ISPs (Tier 1 providers)  and backbone carriers who needed to maintain constant uptime across geographically diverse points of presence.  It can be adapted by large enterprise networks on a smaller scale.  The design assumes each of the primary NAPs or is fully meshed to the others, creating multiple redundant paths that allow traffic to dynamically reroute in the event of a failure at any single node or transit segment. The enhances east–west and north–south resilience by distributing failover responsibilities across regions, not just between a pair. For FedEx, this type of configuration can better accommodate complex logistics and application dependencies that require continuous synchronization between multiple operational and data center hubs. It can also reduce latency imbalances and prevent single points of congestion when multiple business units or divisions rely on cross-region authentication, data replication, and workload distribution.
